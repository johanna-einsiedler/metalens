## Introduction

The GenAI-at-work review extracts, from every included paper, what the paper reports about generative AI and work: the study's design, the units of its estimates — tasks, arms, outcomes, subgroups, horizons — the estimates themselves, read from the paper's exhibits, and the claims of its abstract mapped to the estimates that support them. A pipeline outside Metalens does the extraction and runs its own checks; the record it writes for a paper is what the coders validate here.

The validation has three tasks, each a review template over that one record, each reading the same PDF with the quoted passages highlighted. This workflow is how the three run over one set of papers: one project for the study, one dataset holding the records, the people added to the project, each task assigning its papers to them, the release gated on every verdict.

## Steps

1. **Extraction record** (supplied, not reviewed in place). The pipeline's record of the paper — the tasks with their descriptions, their arms (what each got) and outcomes (what an index is made of), one reference row and one contrast row per estimate with the numbers as printed, the claims of the paper's text mapped to their estimates, its exhibits with their transcribed cells, the flags the pipeline's checks raised and whether it routed the paper — is uploaded with the PDF into the project's one dataset.
2. **Record checks** (deterministic, free). The pipeline's checks ported to run over the record as stored: structure (ids, baselines, contrasts, a second main row), plausibility and scale (signs, non-positive spreads, a per-cent change printed as a proportion or not supported by the arm means, GRIM on integer counts), statistics within the rounding (the interval, the p-value and the printed significance against the standard error, the effect against the string it was read from), SD versus SE, duplicates, and every number against the quote it cites. What needs the exhibit transcriptions, the t-statistic, the text quote or the second run stays in the pipeline and arrives as the flags of a routed paper.
3. **Judgment** (template `genai-work-judgment`, two coders per paper). A coder decides the screening criteria and the study design from the PDF alone — nothing of the model's shown, nothing highlighted on that stage — then confirms the units as the extraction listed them, each with its description and its passage and no number shown, giving every task, arm and outcome its category without seeing the model's choice, then judges for each sentence of the abstract whether it is about the unit the extraction matched it to. Two coders code every paper, each in a coding of their own, blind to the other.
4. **Extraction check** (template `genai-work-check`, one coder per paper). One table or figure of the paper, drawn at random among those an estimate cites — one per paper, the draw a function of the project's seed and the paper, so it never moves. Its transcribed cells are shown with the unit each was mapped to; the coder confirms value and mapping cell by cell, adds the cells the extraction missed, and may flag the paper for a full review.
5. **Routed papers** (template `genai-work-routed`, one coder per paper). A paper is routed when its record contradicts an inclusion criterion (every extracted size below 200, no no-AI reference arm, no performance outcome, no identification), when a headline estimate scores high on the pipeline's checks, or when a coder flagged it. Only routed papers enter this task; every flag — the checks' codes on its headline estimates, the runs' disagreements, the criterion, the coder's flag — is resolved with a verdict and, where it applies, a corrected value.
6. **Contract check** (deterministic, free). The declared fields, types and options of the record are re-checked on the values as stored — after the coders' corrections — so a correction that breaks the shape is found before the release. Findings are notes on the records and in the Workflow panel; they never change a verdict.
7. **Release**. Gated on the record and the three tasks: every assigned coder gave a verdict on every paper of their task, or the owner released anyway, which the release records.

## Review

Every task reviews whole papers (`unit: paper`; the check draws one row of the paper's exhibits). The judgment task assigns each paper to two of the project's people, the other two tasks to one; coders are assigned in turn, so the load is even, and no coder sees another's coding. The project's owner may say, per task, how many of its people review each paper or which ones (the solo pilot runs the judgment with one). A coder's answers to a template's questions, their categories, corrections, verdicts and notes are their coding; the record as uploaded stays as it was. Agreement between the two judgment coders, and between each coder and the pipeline, is computed from the export, which carries every coding with its answers by label.

## Versions

The workflow pins the extraction preset by its data contract and names the three templates. A change to the preset's fields, to a step, to a template's questions or to the review declaration (the number of coders, the draw, the filters included) is a new version of the workflow; a change of wording is not. Version 4 reads the three tasks over the one extraction record; versions 2 and 3 imported one file per pass per paper into three datasets of their own.
